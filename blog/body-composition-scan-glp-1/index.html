Body Composition Scans on a GLP-1: How to Set Your Baseline (Even If You've Already Started)

Kevin Brown, PT, MPT
November 23, 2026 · 13 min read

If you're on a GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) medication like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro or Zepbound, the bathroom scale only tells you part of the story. It tells you that you lost weight. It can't tell you whether you lost fat or muscle. A body composition scan can. Get one now, whether you're about to start or you've been on the medication for months, and repeat it every 4 to 8 weeks.

I'm a physical therapist (PT) of 38 years, and I'm on a very low dose GLP-1 myself. Not for weight loss. My endocrinologist prescribed it to treat prediabetes and for longevity.

That's actually how these medications started. They were developed for diabetes. The first one, exenatide (Byetta), was approved in 2005 for type 2 diabetes. Doctors then noticed how much weight people were losing, and the weight-loss versions followed: Saxenda in 2014, Wegovy in 2021 and Zepbound in 2023. (Mounjaro and Zepbound are technically dual GLP-1/GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) medications, but they're usually grouped with the GLP-1s.)

Here's my twist: I'm not trying to lose weight. I'm fighting to eat enough good food to keep my weight up, and that means fighting to keep my muscle. So I bought an InBody H20N to scan myself at home and keep score. Full disclosure: I own one, I don't sell them, and nobody's paying me to mention it. I'm going to show you my own scans, including two that were flat-out wrong, because it'll teach you more than any study will.

Why the scale lies on a GLP-1

When you lose weight on a GLP-1, a big chunk of it can be lean mass. In the major trials, roughly 25% to 40% of the weight people lost wasn't fat. I covered that in detail in how much muscle you can lose on a GLP-1.

Here's the problem. Two people can both lose 15 pounds and look identical on the scale. One lost 13 pounds of fat. The other lost 9 pounds of fat and 6 pounds of muscle. The first person is healthier, stronger and has better blood sugar control. The second is weaker and more likely to regain the fat. The scale gives them both a gold star.

A body composition scan splits your weight into its parts, so you know which person you are.

Lean body mass vs. muscle mass: what the scan actually measures

Lean body mass is everything in your body that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs, skin and water. Skeletal muscle mass is just the muscle you can train, the muscle attached to your bones that moves you. It's a subset of lean mass, usually a bit more than half of it.

Number on your scanWhat it isWhat you want on a GLP-1
Body fat massPounds of fatGoing down
Skeletal muscle massPounds of trainable muscleHolding steady or going up
Lean body massEverything that isn't fatHolding as steady as possible

One thing worth knowing: a lot of the "lean mass" people lose in the first few weeks on a GLP-1 is water, not muscle. Eating less means storing less water. So don't panic over one early drop. Watch the trend.

Already on a GLP-1? Your first scan is your baseline

Most of what you'll read online says to get a scan before you start the medication. That's ideal. But if you're already a few months in, you didn't miss your chance.

Your first scan is your baseline. From that day forward, you'll know whether you're losing fat, muscle or both, and you can do something about it. The muscle you protect from here on counts just as much as the muscle you would have protected if you'd started earlier.

So don't wait. Get scanned.

InBody vs. DEXA: which body composition scan should you get?

There are two main options most people can get to.

DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) uses a very low dose of X-ray to measure fat, lean tissue and bone. It's the most accurate body composition scan most people can actually book, and community providers now charge around $40 to $60. One catch: DEXA's "lean" number includes water and organs, not just muscle. That's why muscle researchers are moving toward MRI, CT, and a newer test called D3-creatine dilution, where you swallow a capsule of specially labeled creatine and give a urine sample.

InBody uses BIA (bioelectrical impedance analysis). You stand on metal plates and hold handles while a tiny, painless electrical current runs through you. Water and muscle conduct electricity well. Fat doesn't. The machine uses that difference to estimate what you're made of.

My take: if you can afford one DEXA scan as a starting point, great. But for tracking month to month, an InBody done the right way is the more practical tool, because you'll actually repeat it. A scan you get once and never repeat doesn't show you a trend.

What about home scales? Most bathroom "smart scales" only touch your feet, so the current runs up one leg and down the other and the scale guesses at the rest of you. Home units with handles, like the InBody H20N I use, send the current through your hands and feet (eight contact points), so they measure your arms and trunk too. That's a real step up. Either way, a home unit is only as good as your consistency, which is the next section.

How accurate is InBody? Here are my own scans

Research comparing InBody to DEXA finds it tracks well across groups of people but can be off by several percentage points for any one person. In one study of 226 adults, InBody read body fat about 3 percentage points lower than DEXA on average. For me, it's the opposite: my InBody reads about 3 points higher than my last DEXA (12.6% vs. 9.6%). Yet the two agreed almost exactly on my fat-free mass, about 130 pounds. The error goes different directions for different people, which is one more reason never to compare numbers from two different machines.

The bigger issue isn't accuracy against DEXA. It's consistency from one scan to the next. Here are my own InBody scans from the last two weeks:

Look at November 12. According to the machine, in three days I gained almost 2 pounds of muscle and lost 5 pounds of fat. Nobody does that. I had just come out of the sauna. Heat brings blood flow to your skin, and that lets the electrical current pass more easily, which the machine reads as more lean tissue and less fat. I was actually 1.8 pounds lighter from sweating, and the scan still told me I'd built muscle.

So I tested it again. On November 22, I scanned in the morning, then again that evening right after the sauna. Same day. The evening scan said I'd gained 2.4 pounds of muscle and lost 6.7 pounds of fat, dropping from 12% to 7.6% body fat, while I was almost 3 pounds lighter from sweating. Twice now, same result. It's not a fluke. Heat fools the machine every time.

Now look at November 21. Same body, same day, and the morning and evening scans are almost a pound apart on muscle. After a full day of eating and drinking, the numbers drift.

Then look at the two mornings, November 21 and 22. Same time, same routine, and my muscle reading was identical: 72.3 pounds both days. That's what consistency buys you.

None of this means InBody is useless. It means one scan isn't a baseline, and scans taken under different conditions can't be compared. Control the conditions and the trend becomes trustworthy.

How to get a reliable reading

Do these every single time:

  • Same time of day. Morning is best.
  • Before eating. Give it at least 2 to 3 hours after food.
  • Before exercise. Not after a workout, a run, or a hard walk.
  • No sauna, hot tub or hot shower beforehand. You saw what that did to my numbers.
  • No alcohol the day before.
  • Normal fluids. Don't chug water right before, don't show up dehydrated.
  • Empty your bladder first.
  • Women: try to scan at the same point in your cycle each time, because water retention shifts readings.

For your baseline, take 2 or 3 scans this way over a week or two and average them. That average is your starting point. Averaging my three usable scans puts my baseline at 148 pounds, 72.1 pounds of skeletal muscle, 18.6 pounds of fat and 12.6% body fat.

What to track on a GLP-1, and how often

You don't need to understand every number on the printout. Watch these:

  1. Skeletal muscle mass. The one that matters most on a GLP-1.
  2. Body fat mass. In pounds, not just percentage.
  3. Visceral fat level. The fat around your organs, which drives metabolic risk.

Judge your progress every 4 to 8 weeks. Real change in that window is bigger than the normal day-to-day wobble you saw in my numbers. Scanning more often is fine, even helpful, as long as you average the readings. Extra scans make it easy to spot and toss the oddballs, like my sauna readings.

What to do if your scan shows muscle loss

Two things move the needle, and you need both.

Protein. Your scan gives you your lean body mass, and that sets your protein target. I use about 1 gram of protein per pound of lean body mass a day. My lean mass is about 129 pounds, so I aim for about 130 grams. More on that in how much protein to eat on a GLP-1.

Strength training that actually challenges you. Walking is great, but it won't hold onto muscle by itself. Your muscles need a reason to stay, and that reason is lifting something heavy enough that the last couple of reps feel like work, two to three times a week, and progressing over time.

Take the challenge, and bring a partner

First, what's realistic? The largest review of adults 50 and older (49 studies, more than 1,300 people) found that people who strength trained gained about 2.4 pounds (1.1 kilograms) of lean mass on average, usually over about 4 to 5 months. Programs with more total work produced bigger gains, and older participants gained a bit less. Younger adults and true beginners tend to gain faster, and that matches what I've seen in 38 years of treating patients. But the gains are real at any age. They're just slower than the internet promises.

Here's my challenge, in two tracks:

  • Steady weight, not on a GLP-1: gain 2 pounds of muscle. Under 50 and new to lifting, give yourself 3 months. Over 50, give yourself 4 to 5.
  • On a GLP-1 and losing weight: lose the fat, keep every pound of muscle. Holding steady while the scale drops is a win.

Because 2 pounds is close to the normal day-to-day wobble on a scan, judge it by the average of 2 or 3 clean scans, not one reading.

  1. Get your baseline. Either buy a home unit with hand and foot electrodes, or book a DEXA scan. Use the prep rules above and average 2 or 3 scans.
  2. Find a partner. A spouse, a friend, a training buddy. Scan together, train together, and check in with each other. It's a lot harder to skip a workout when someone's waiting on you.
  3. Train and eat your protein. Strength train 2 to 3 times a week and hit your protein target.
  4. Rescan at least every 4 weeks, under the same conditions, and compare your averages.

So I'm about as far from a beginner as you get, and I'm on the slow end of every one of those research findings. Frequently asked questions

Can you eat before an InBody scan?

It's best not to. Food and drink in your stomach add weight and change how the current moves through you. Scan in the morning, at least 2 to 3 hours after eating, and do it the same way every time.

How does alcohol affect an InBody scan?

Alcohol shifts your body water, which throws off the reading. It's best to skip alcohol the day before a scan so your results are comparable to your last one.

Should I get an InBody scan during my period?

Water retention around your period can shift the numbers. You can still scan, but for the cleanest trend, scan at the same point in your cycle each time.

How often should you get a body composition scan on a GLP-1?

Judge your progress every 4 to 8 weeks, because real changes in muscle and fat take weeks to show up. You can scan more often than that. Just average the readings, so the odd ones don't fool you, and don't judge progress from one scan to the next.

Is a DEXA scan worth it for weight loss?

If you can afford it, one DEXA scan is a great starting point, and it also checks your bone density. For tracking month to month, a consistently done InBody scan is cheaper and easier to repeat, and repeating is what shows you the trend.

How much does a body composition scan cost?

InBody scans at San Diego gyms run about $15 to $25, and some gyms include them free with a membership. DEXA scans at community providers run about $40 to $60. I paid $100 for two DEXA scans. If you want to scan often, a home unit with hand and foot electrodes costs roughly $225 to $300 and pays for itself in about a year of monthly gym scans.

The bottom line

The scale tells you how much you weigh. A body composition scan tells you what you're made of, and on a GLP-1 that's the number that matters. Get your baseline now, even if you've already started. Scan the same way every time. Watch the trend, not one reading.

And if muscle is slipping, stick with progressive strength training and eat your protein. Both are a stimulus. Training is the big one, and its signal lasts a day or two. Protein sends its own build signal and supplies the building material, but its signal fades in a few hours and gets weaker with age, so older adults need a bigger serving at each meal, about 25 to 40 grams.

This article is for general education and isn't medical advice. Don't change your medication without talking to your prescriber. If you have a pacemaker or other implanted electronic device, or you're pregnant, don't use a BIA scanner like InBody; ask your doctor about alternatives.
